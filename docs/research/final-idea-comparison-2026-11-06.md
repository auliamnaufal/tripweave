## Executive summary and recommendation

**No candidate is an unqualified winner.** For a six-day, software-only build in which agent judgment and real PayPal relevance both matter, I recommend a **narrow, human-approved scope-change copilot for an independent commission-based creative** as the conditional first choice. It compares a new client request with the actual agreed brief, cites the evidence, and asks the artist a useful question when meaning is unclear. The artist—not the model—sets price and approves a draft/sent invoice for a named add-on. A client then pays that artist through PayPal Invoicing. This is a stronger connection between agent judgment and the transaction than an app selling an unrelated “AI report.” But it is not proven novel or demanded, VGen is a real competitor, and the demo is only an honest freelancer payment if the seller/payee really is the freelancer’s authorized sandbox merchant account. If that account/invoice buyer flow cannot be proven promptly, do not pretend entrant-owned proceeds belong to the freelancer; switch to an entrant-owned service transaction and re-score PayPal centrality.

**Shortlist:** (1) scope-change copilot; (2) used-car document discrepancy explainer; (3) tabletop-miniature project-kit planner. These occupy different domains and expose the decision trade-off: strongest PayPal-to-user-job connection (scope-change), strongest primary-source evidence and inspectable consequence (used car), versus most distinct constrained product-agent demo (hobby kit). If the artist workflow or merchant setup fails validation, used-car is the best bounded alternative for a clean three-minute sandbox Checkout demo, but it charges for the app's own report and does not pay a dealer. Hobby planning is a good Channel3 experiment, **not** third-party shopping checkout; that distinction makes PayPal less central.

The recommendation is **not** to revive “AI that can buy from any site.” PayPal Orders v2 is a merchant/partner order API: buyer approves a specific order, then the merchant's server captures it. It does not debit a third-party retailer or create that retailer's order. Channel3's current checkout page says checkout is “coming soon” and describes product discovery, retailer links and commissions today. [S2–S4] The broader hackathon pass/fail test requires PayPal to be central and a working prototype; the five scored criteria are equally weighted, so mere novelty or a visually dramatic fake purchase is not enough. [S1]

**Practical next action:** spend the first half-day validating that one artist sandbox Business account can create/send an invoice and a sandbox Personal account can pay it, and test 10 clear/ambiguous scope cases with a working artist. Continue only if no included work is falsely marked billable, evidence is traceable, and the invoice path represents the correct seller. The local six-day clock makes integration proof—not another broad brainstorm—the gating experiment.

## 1. What “best hackathon idea” means here: reframe + constraints

### Reframe the objective

A common framing—“which idea is newest / has the most wow?”—overweights novelty and enables a polished simulation to masquerade as a product. Reframe as **which smallest, testable workflow earns the greatest combined credibility per remaining day while surviving the hard rules?** A good candidate should (a) make an actual named user's consequential ambiguity visible, (b) leave irreversible money and safety decisions under user/merchant authority, (c) have an honest named PayPal seller, buyer, item and fulfillment obligation, (d) fit a functioning three-minute demo, and (e) expose a falsifiable uncertainty that can be tested now.

This is a weighted-goals problem with **non-compensatory constraints**. First require software-only feasibility, meaningful AI and PayPal, a working demo, truthful transaction claims, and no unacceptable financial/safety/legal action without approval. Then score the survivors. A high impact score cannot compensate for an inaccessible payment rail; a beautiful payment screen cannot compensate for a non-existent seller or an LLM that cannot explain its judgment. This mirrors the official pass/fail viability gate, followed by equal weighting for implementation, design, impact, innovation and presentation. [S1]

### Preserve the tension: agent judgment versus deterministic execution

Do not resolve this tension by making the LLM both “the whole agent” and the calculator, payment authority, merchant, or final safety decision-maker. The promising pattern is:

- **Agent judges:** interpret inconsistent natural-language context, decide what evidence is relevant, surface competing interpretations, ask the smallest high-value clarification, and abstain when the record does not support an answer.
- **Ordinary code does:** validate identity/state, use fixed thresholds, price, totals, taxes, eligibility gates, schedules, arithmetic, idempotency, permissions, and transitions. PayPal—not a language model—reports payment status.
- **Human approves:** external messages, invoice send/capture when appropriate, refunds, payout releases, event safety decisions, vehicle decisions, and any consequential recommendation.

A deterministic workflow can still be a useful product; it just should not be sold as deep agentic judgment. A genuine ambiguity is not license for a model to invent authority. In the opposite direction, a model that merely paraphrases a fixed rule is superficial AI wrapping.

### Search adjacent possibilities, not just commerce

The researched portfolio includes creator contracting, group finance/payroll, shopping/procurement, vehicle paperwork, bids, charity allocation, accessibility, care, repair, weather/event operations, childcare, education, and remittance. The breadth matters: a compelling decision task can exist without a credible PayPal product path, and a usable payment API does not itself make the problem worth solving. The saved taste file specifically favors real agent judgment, first-party competitor research, software-only ideas, payment-journey completeness, broad-domain search, and investigating crowded/simple ideas rather than defending them reflexively. [L1]

### Invert each premise

- **“AI recommendations are needed.”** What if search, official guidance, or a calculator already suffices? Then favor the cases where a user has conflicting artifacts, hidden dependencies, evolving constraints, or must choose which ambiguity to resolve (scope-change, used-car docs, bids, access itinerary). Drop generic gift/product discovery and summary-only ideas.
- **“Payment must be central.”** What if it must be the named user's real transaction, not a sandbox button after advice? That favors a real artist-client invoice or organizer's own ticket. It weakens the app-paid report candidates, but does not make them dishonest if clearly sold as the app's actual report. It rules out calling an entrant's Orders capture a retailer, daycare, dealer, charity, or repair-shop payment.
- **“Autonomy is more impressive.”** What if trust, consent, account limits and reversibility matter more than speed? The right agent proposes, explains, gets approval, and executes one bounded action. Approval, cancel, decline, timeout and refund are product features—not demo nuisances.
- **“The concept is novel because no exact competitor was found.”** What if that means no demand, poor distribution, or a search gap? Treat competitor absence as an uncertainty to test, not evidence of whitespace.
- **“More AI means more innovation.”** Invert: remove AI. If the differentiator and user outcome remain unchanged (split arithmetic, invoice reminder templates, list sorting), AI is cosmetic. If the tool no longer handles contextual interpretation, trade-offs, targeted clarification, or abstention (scope-change; constrained car paperwork; caregiver/event contingencies), there may be genuine judgment—subject to benchmark and user proof.

## 3. PayPal flow: what is real, what is not, and who owns the outcome

### Standard own-merchant Orders Checkout

For **the app's own fixed-price digital report/service**, the accurate flow is: merchant server stores the product, price, currency and session/order association → server obtains OAuth token and creates an Orders v2 order → buyer reviews and approves that named merchant/order in PayPal → buyer returns → merchant server captures **after buyer approval** → application checks capture status before unlocking delivery. PayPal's current guide explicitly says server-side Orders API calls, approval, then capture; `CAPTURE` is the normal direct case. An authorization-and-later-capture path is for stock/shipping confirmation (the authorization is held for up to 29 days), not needed for a fixed digital report. [S2]

Sandbox is documented for testing: developer accounts have separate Personal buyer and Business seller test accounts; sandbox transactions are simulated and do not prove live eligibility, settlement or demand. PayPal says US integrations may be tested with a Developer account, while going live or testing outside the US requires a PayPal Business account. Secrets stay server-side. [S3]

### Invoices are a named seller-to-customer bill, not marketplace onboarding

Invoicing v2 can create a draft, send/schedule an invoice to a customer, remind/cancel it, inspect it and record payment/refund. The invoice is issued by the API caller's merchant account to a named client. It is an apt primitive for the artist's own add-on work or collective lead's own client invoice **only if that account is actually the seller and has authority**. It does not turn entrant credentials into an artist account or pay a freelancer's own account by magic. [S5]

### Retailer checkout and marketplace/partner onboarding are different

If Channel3 returns retailer X's offer/buy link, the shopper goes to X, places an order there, and pays X at X's checkout; X fulfills, supports, refunds and handles its sale. If entrant Y creates an ordinary PayPal Orders order, the buyer pays Y for Y's own named item/service; that transaction neither charges X nor submits an order to X. Channel3 currently says checkout is coming soon. [S4]

PayPal's current marketplace/platform guide says Complete Payments Platform is **only available to approved partners**, requires the platform to submit its business/use case for PayPal review/approval, and allows sandbox testing before approval; seller onboarding is a defined part of platform payments. This is the evidence needed to reject the claim that an entrant can simply set a retailer's `payee` or collect for any freelancer/merchant. A normal account is not a substitute for seller onboarding/consent. [S25]

### Invoices, Payouts, Xoom and Giving Fund are not interchangeable

- **Invoice:** seller/payee is the invoicing merchant and sells a specified service/good to a named buyer.
- **Payouts:** a business disbursement to named eligible recipients; it is not consumer remittance, not a general checkout, and is not automatically legal payroll or escrow. Bagi's local evidence notes a specific Payouts Agreement payroll restriction—must verify before any live employee payout. [L3]
- **Xoom:** a consumer remittance product. No public self-serve Xoom transaction API for a hackathon entrant was established in reviewed materials; do not substitute Payouts or Orders and call it a Xoom transfer.
- **PayPal Giving Fund:** its partner page offers Charity Search API access and says partners integrate to support charities; it invites prospective platforms to apply. It does not prove a hackathon entrant's access/approval in six days. [S15]
- **Agent Toolkit/MCP:** merchant-side tool exposure for documented PayPal actions; it is not consumer authorization to spend, does not grant arbitrary seller/retailer access, and should not be connected with broad write tools without explicit controls. Current repository landscape warns older commerce tools are remote/feature-flagged or unverified for entrants. [S38, L4]

### Lifecycle and failure handling is part of the product

For every real flow, include these states; do not show just a green “paid” screen:

1. **Created, not approved:** no money moved; invoice/order is not payment success.
2. **Buyer approval:** buyer sees exact seller, item/amount and approves. A user closing/canceling the PayPal flow must return to an unpaid/cancelled state; don't capture an unapproved order.
3. **Declined / approval failed / capture failed:** show unpaid, do not fulfill or release downstream work/payout; allow a controlled retry or alternative payment path.
4. **Capture response lost/timeout:** reconcile current status and retry only the same logical supported POST with the same `PayPal-Request-Id`; PayPal returns latest status for stored idempotency IDs, but not every API supports that header. Never create a new capture just because the browser timed out. [S39]
5. **Webhook:** subscribe to relevant app events, verify signature, acknowledge 2xx, dedupe event IDs and make state transitions idempotent. PayPal retries unsuccessful webhook delivery up to 25 times over three days; events may reflect dashboard actions, including refunds. [S40]
6. **After capture cancellation:** capture is not “undone.” The merchant applies its disclosed service cancellation policy and initiates a full/partial refund against the capture ID, records the result and reconciles webhooks/dashboard changes. [S41]
7. **Dispute:** the selling merchant owns buyer communication, evidence and dispute outcome. PayPal's Disputes API supports lifecycle actions; accepting a claim closes in buyer's favor and triggers a refund. Do not represent a refund mock or dispute demo as processed. [S42]
8. **Explicit money-action approval:** creating an invoice draft is lower impact than sending it; sending, capture, refund, accepting a claim, or payout needs the appropriate authorized merchant/human confirmation. Model suggestions cannot change amount/payee or grant consent.

### When PayPal is cosmetic

PayPal is cosmetic when a recommendation ends with an unrelated app fee, when the payment funds an entrant account but the demo claims a retailer/artist/charity received it, when an event ticket is invented solely to show Checkout, or when a fake seller “accepts” then the app's own merchant captures money. An own-service sale can be honest, but centrality is weaker unless the service itself is valuable and the payment is a real part of how that target gets the service. An actual freelancer invoice or actual organizer-owned registration is stronger only when the seller's identity and fulfillment are real in the demo's defined sandbox scenario.

## 5. Recommended MVP: scope-change copilot

### Exact user and promise

**User:** independent illustrator/designer who takes direct commission work outside an all-in-one platform and has a short original written brief plus a client request in messages.

**Promise:** “Before you reply, see whether this new request is covered by what you agreed, what sentence supports that view, and what to ask if the terms are unclear.” Do not promise legal interpretation, successful negotiation, guaranteed earnings or automatic invoicing.

### Three-minute end-to-end demo

1. Load two synthetic cases; explicitly label data fictional. Case A asks for a revision within a two-revision agreement. Agent cites the clause and recommends **no additional charge**.
2. Case B asks for a new background absent from the agreed deliverables. Agent cites the scope section, labels “likely outside scope / confirm,” distinguishes “not stated” from “explicitly excluded,” and asks the artist for their deadline/price instead of inventing either.
3. Artist edits the proposed add-on, selects an artist-entered fixed amount and reviews a polite change message. Show a counterfactual: changing the agreement from “one revision” to “two” changes Case A's classification.
4. Artist explicitly approves **Create draft invoice**, inspects exact payee/client/description/amount, then separately approves **Send invoice**. Client uses the sandbox PayPal experience and approves payment. Verify final invoice/payment state from PayPal, not animation. The seller/payee displayed must be the sandbox freelancer account actually issuing the invoice; a sandbox Personal buyer pays it.
5. Show cancel/decline branch as unpaid, no work-start flag; if a refund is included, actually issue and reconcile one sandbox refund or label it visibly as simulation. No real DMs, live charges, or automatic communication.

### MVP boundaries and stop conditions

- Keep only one artist, one sandbox seller, one client, two synthetic cases, one invoice, one payment; no email/DM import, marketplace API, tax, subscriptions, stored cards, or customer billing.
- Evidence spans are shown and editable. `included`, `likely_outside`, `ambiguous` are suggestions, not verdicts. Contradictory/absent terms -> ask/abstain.
- Artist provides amount and terms; deterministic code builds item lines and invoice amount. Model may not alter payee or invoke financial write action without explicit authorization.
- If only entrant-owned sandbox seller credentials work, item must say the entrant sells a specific, actually fulfilled service; do not claim a freelancer received funds. If that makes PayPal peripheral, stop and reselect rather than retell it.
- If payment path is not functional by **8 November**, reduce scope to tested invoice creation/payment and one judgment example. If no invoice can be paid from a sandbox buyer or the artist's account identity cannot be made truthful, pivot to the used-car report demo only after a real own-merchant checkout works. No production/marketplace integration is assumed.

## 6. Go/no-go tests and failure-mode checklist

### First-day validation tests

1. **Merchant identity/payment gate:** test sandbox Business merchant, draft invoice, send, Personal buyer approval/payment, and merchant-side final status. Record seller identity, exact item, transaction IDs, and whether invoice status matches payment. Test close/cancel and payment failure. No claim of artist receipt if the credentials belong to the entrant.
2. **Judgment benchmark:** create 10 hand-authored, artist-reviewed scenarios: clearly included, clearly extra, ambiguous, contradictory, and missing clause. Require cited evidence for every outside-scope label; no confident billable result for included or unresolved terms. Any confident false-charge recommendation blocks release.
3. **User evidence:** five 15-minute interviews with artists/designers taking direct commissions. Ask them to reconstruct a recent anonymized case and present original agreement/request (with consent), existing workflow, error cost, competitor/tool use, desired automation boundary, and usefulness of a cited classification. Do not lead with “would you use AI?” or treat stated willingness to pay as proof.
4. **Compete test:** show actual VGen commission workflow and ask whether the target artist works outside it and what unresolved task remains. If VGen solves their actual issue or their commission terms are consistently clear, stop.
5. **Failure/state test:** duplicate button click, repeated invoice send attempt, timeout after create/send/capture, webhook duplicate/out-of-order, cancellation, decline, later refund. Assert one invoice/payment state, no unpaid unlock, no silent retry to a different order, correct recovery message.
6. **Demo-readiness:** run the exact 3-minute path on the submission device/network. A recorded/live failure should be clearly explained, not replaced with a fake success state.

### Stop / pivot gates

- **Stop the recommendation** if no one in five recent target artists can provide a relevant case, if all have effective existing processes, or if artist review finds a harmful false billable classification.
- **Narrow to ask-only** if evidence extraction is unstable but targeted clarification is useful; do not issue invoices based on model conclusions.
- **Pivot to used-car** only if it passes a zero-critical-false-reassurance test on the small demo set, source citations are explicit and sandbox checkout works for the report. A small synthetic sample cannot establish real safety.
- **Do not pivot to Haggler, Bagi, or collective payouts** merely because they are more dramatic: their unproven seller/money authority, legal/payout constraints, excluded scope, or agent-judgment deficits remain.

### Risk checklist by class

- [ ] Model cannot change amount, currency, merchant/payee, customer, capture/refund/payout recipient or consent state.
- [ ] Every classification traces to the specific original agreement/request. Absence of a clause is not evidence of an exclusion.
- [ ] Ambiguity routes to a question; all messages and invoices remain unsent until human approval.
- [ ] Synthetic examples only for demo; private client information is not retained by default; provide deletion and disclose model processing.
- [ ] No legal, tax, dispute or contract-enforceability advice claim.
- [ ] Sandbox configuration is visibly labeled; secrets remain server-side; no live credentials, vaulted payment methods or recurring charges.
- [ ] Order/invoice is checked server-side; API idempotency used where supported; webhooks are signature-verified and deduplicated.
- [ ] Buyer cancel/decline/timeout never means paid; paid cancellation goes through the actual seller's refund policy and PayPal refund path.
- [ ] Seller handles delivery, support, refund, chargeback and dispute; an entrant app does not imply these obligations belong to PayPal.
- [ ] No retailer website login automation, third-party merchant impersonation, arbitrary `payee`, “PayPal paid the artist” unless seller/payee confirms it, or claims that Channel3 reserves inventory.
- [ ] If hobby fallback: source/refresh price and availability, label retailer link-out, verify SKU and compatibility against curated rules, label shipping/tax as unknown, disclose affiliate links if applicable.
- [ ] If used-car fallback: distinguish seller assertions from official guidance; do not say “safe,” “fair price,” or “best loan”; never infer clean recall/history from missing data; minimize financing PII.

## 7. Conclusion: recommendation, not certainty

The strongest strategy is not “build the most autonomous buyer” or “attach Checkout to any socially valuable task.” It is **make one inspectable judgment consequential in a real user's flow, keep money movement deterministic and explicitly authorized, then prove one honest payment path.** The scope copilot best meets that bar in the current repo's evidence and the builder's stated preferences, but it survives only as a conditional hypothesis. Its first two go/no-go gates are an actual correct-seller sandbox invoice journey and an artist-tested non-harmful classification. If either fails, the evidence favors a cautious used-car report experiment over a simulated Haggler transaction or pretending an entrant's Orders payment completed a retailer sale. If even an entrant-owned digital-service fee is judged too peripheral, the right next move is a verified merchant-partner test or a fresh payment-native concept—not a fictional retailer, artist or seller.

The canonical research reports and PRDs remain history, including incompatible earlier choices. This document is a new synthesis for a decision; it does not replace or retroactively validate them.
